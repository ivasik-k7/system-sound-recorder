# Code of conduct

## The standard

Be decent to people. Assume the person you are replying to is competent and
acting in good faith, and that they know something you do not.

That covers nearly everything. The rest is detail for the cases where it does
not.

## Expected

- Take the technical argument seriously and leave the person out of it.
- Say what you observed and how you know. "This drops samples on my machine
  under load, here is the manifest" is worth more than "this is broken".
- Accept that a maintainer may say no to a change that works. Scope is a real
  constraint.
- Accept correction. Being wrong in public is the ordinary cost of working in
  public.

## Not accepted

- Harassment, insults, and personal or political attacks.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Sustained disruption: reopening a settled decision indefinitely, or making
  every thread about the same grievance.

## Recordings, specifically

This project is about recording audio, and that gives conduct here a concrete
edge that most projects do not have.

**Do not attach or link recordings of identifiable people** in issues, pull
requests or discussions, even to demonstrate a bug, and even if you were part of
the conversation. The other participants did not agree to have it posted on the
internet. Use a tone, silence, or your own voice.

**Do not use this project's spaces to ask for help recording someone without
their knowledge.** Questions about the tool are welcome. Requests for help
concealing a recording from the people in it are not, and will be closed.

## Scope

This applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, code review — and to anyone representing the project elsewhere.

## Enforcement

Report a problem privately to the maintainers, through GitHub. Reports are
handled confidentially, and the person reporting is not required to have a thick
skin about it.

Responses, in rough order: a private word, a public correction, editing or
removing the content, a temporary block, a permanent one. Which one depends on
severity and on whether it is a pattern. Maintainers who behave this way are
held to it more firmly rather than less.
